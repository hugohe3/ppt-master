# HK Client Pitch — Style Specification

> Method and design defaults only. No project communication contract, brand identity, page structure, or SVG prototypes.

## I. Style Overview

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Style Name | HK Client Pitch |
| Best Fit | Hong Kong client proposals, vendor finals, partnership pitches, and 30–45 minute live briefings that must hold a bilingual room and leave a signable next step |
| Reusable Intent | Earn the room in the first minute, prove the client's situation is understood, then make one specific plan, proof, and ask scannable both live and when the file is forwarded overnight |
| Sources | Authored for this Hong Kong fork, 2026-10-28, from Hong Kong client-meeting practice (hook → problem → insight → solution → proof → ask, reserved Q&A, follow-up with action items). Not a transcription of any firm's proprietary pitch system |

## II. Communication Method

- **Preferred Mode**: custom
- **Mode References**: pyramid, showcase
- **Mode Behavior**: Open like a showcase — one hook the room can see in the first thirty seconds — then resolve like a pyramid: the client's problem in the client's terms, the insight that changes the options, the recommended approach, the proof, and a single ask. Keep the live meeting inside a declared time box and hold the last minutes for questions. When the file will be forwarded after the meeting, every page must still survive without the presenter.
- **Argument Flow**: Hook with a decision-relevant fact or the client's own tension, restate the problem, offer the insight, unfold the solution in three to five modules, prove it with comparable evidence, and close with the specific next action (NDA, pilot, mandate, board paper). Do not open with company history, team photos, or a long credentials parade.
- **Page Message Discipline**: Give each page one promise, one module of the approach, or one piece of proof, titled as the client's outcome rather than a capability name. When Traditional Chinese and English both appear, one language owns the title and body; the other is a support line at 0.75–0.9×, never a mirrored second column. Keep the two scripts on separate lines or separate objects.
- **Claim Discipline**: Keep understood requirement, proposed commitment, assumption, exclusion, and optional scope explicitly distinct. Never imply a capability, certification, reference, or relationship that cannot be produced on request. Present proof with its real scope. The closing page is an ask with owner and date, not thanks.

## III. Page Role Vocabulary

| Role | Communication Job | Evidence Obligation | Composition Tendency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Put the stake on the table in the first glance | Use a client-relevant fact, tension, or number — not an adjective | One dominant line; almost no competing chrome |
| Situation understanding | Show the client's reality is genuinely understood | Use the client's own facts and language from discovery, not a generic industry sketch | Lead with their situation; keep vendor presence minimal |
| Insight | State what changes the options | Tie the insight to evidence the client can inspect | Make the insight the page; keep the supporting fact adjacent |
| Recommended approach | State the plan and why it fits this client | Connect each module to a specific requirement or constraint | Make the recommendation dominant and its rationale visible |
| Solution module | Show one part of the approach as an outcome | Give the mechanism, boundary, and what the client gets | One module per page; avoid capability grids |
| Proof and reference | Establish this has been done before | Give comparable work with scope, scale, outcome, and permission | One relevant reference at real depth beats a logo wall |
| Commercial terms | State what it costs and what triggers payment | Give price basis, assumptions, inclusions, exclusions, and validity | Keep the number and its basis together |
| Risk and dependency | Bound the promise honestly | Pair each real risk or client dependency with the response | Keep risk and response in direct correspondence |
| Ask / next step | Make the decision easy to act on | Give the specific action, owner, and date that moves to pilot or mandate | One dominant action; reserve a Q&A window rather than filling it |
| Leave-behind detail | Survive overnight forwarding | Retain the argument, numbers, and conditions without narration | Allow higher density only after the live spine is clear |

## IV. Evidence & Data Expression

- **Argument Trace**: Every commitment traces to a client requirement, and every capability claim traces to evidence that can be produced in diligence. Where the response depends on a hire, a partner, or a client action, say so.
- **Charts**: Use charts for the client's own data, projected outcomes, and delivery sequence. State the basis of any projected benefit on the chart itself, distinguish modeled from measured, and keep comparison conditions matched. Never present a benefit curve without the assumptions that generate it.
- **Tables**: Use tables for scope inclusion and exclusion, pricing, and responsibility. Mark optional and conditional items clearly, state units, `HK$` or other declared currency, and validity period, and make the boundary between included and excluded impossible to misread.
- **Sources**: Attribute client-supplied facts to their discovery source, cite third-party benchmarks with date and scope, and reference prior work only with permission and accurate scope.
- **Native Editability**: Prefer editable native tables and charts for pricing, scope, and schedule — pitches are revised after the meeting. Keep architecture and process as editable shapes so a scope change can be redrawn rather than rebuilt.

## V. Visual System Defaults

- **Preferred Visual Style**: swiss-minimal
- **Composition**: Build each page around one live beat. Give the hook, recommendation, and ask room; let proof and commercial pages carry structured density. Never split the page into equal bilingual columns. Keep a stable position for the outcome line so a forwarded file still navigates by eye.
- **Density**: Live pages stay speakable inside the time box; leave-behind pages may tighten. A pitch that needs more than a few solution modules is several conversations, not one denser slide.
- **Decoration**: Calm and professional. Hairline structure, restrained emphasis, no handshake stock, no decorative harbour skyline, no gradient banners, no Mainland SOE chrome. Nothing should look more expensive than the work being proposed.
- **Color Behavior**: Keep a light professional field and reserve accent for the client's outcome and the ask. Where a table distinguishes included, optional, and excluded scope, encode that distinction with label and position as well as color. Any confirmed Brand or Deck identity replaces these tendencies.
- **Typography Character**: Use a clear professional sans-serif suited to Traditional Chinese with a Latin companion. Titles short enough to speak; support-language lines quieter. Do not set Chinese in a Latin display face or apply English tracking to Chinese. Exact families remain current-project or resolved identity decisions.

## VI. Image & Icon Direction

- **Preferred Image Rendering**: corporate-photo
- **Image Usage**: Use imagery where it carries the client's context or real delivery evidence — their environment, a comparable installation, a delivered artifact, or the actual team. Never use generic business imagery, stock harbour views, or handshake photography to pad a thin pitch.
- **Image Treatment**: Crop to the subject that supports the point, keep any embedded interface or document text legible, and caption with context and permission status. Avoid full-bleed decorative imagery and synthetic text inside generated images.
- **Icon Treatment**: Use one coherent stroke family at consistent weight to mark recurring classes such as phase, role, or scope status. Never let an icon alone carry an inclusion or exclusion that has commercial consequence.

## VII. Review Focus

> Apply this section only after the user explicitly activates visual review. It never triggers that stage.

- The hook is readable in the first glance and does not depend on narration.
- One language dominates; the second language is support, not a mirrored translation.
- Traditional Chinese glyphs are used wherever Chinese is present unless the audience is explicitly Mainland.
- The client's situation appears before vendor credentials.
- Included, optional, and excluded scope is unmistakable without relying on color alone.
- Price appears together with its basis, assumptions, and validity.
- The closing page is a concrete ask with owner and date, plus a real Q&A window.
- Live pages remain speakable inside a 30–45 minute meeting with time held for questions.
