# HK Bilingual Board — Style Specification

> Method and design defaults only. No project communication contract, brand identity, page structure, or SVG prototypes.

## I. Style Overview

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Style Name | HK Bilingual Board |
| Best Fit | Hong Kong board papers, EXCO or committee decision notes, listed-company IR and results readouts, and time-boxed executive briefings that must be readable in Traditional Chinese and English |
| Reusable Intent | Make each page deliver one decision-relevant answer with visible proof, a clear language hierarchy, and a meeting that can finish on time, while leaving identity, geometry, and the current communication contract open to the project |
| Sources | Authored for this Hong Kong fork, 2026-10-28, from Hong Kong workplace briefing practice (key-message-first / BLUF, bilingual live rooms, strict time boxes) and bilingual slide-typography practice (one language dominates; the second supports). Not a transcription of any issuer, adviser, or government template |

## II. Communication Method

- **Preferred Mode**: pyramid
- **Argument Flow**: Open with the governing decision or the period result, the overall answer, and the time the room will spend on it. Support the answer with one to three proof points, then the request, risks, and owned next steps. Maintain the trace `overall answer → key support → page message → evidence`. Do not open with a long background narrative; Hong Kong executive rooms treat preamble as delay.
- **Page Message Discipline**: For every planned page, identify one governing question and answer it in an assertion title. When the page carries both Traditional Chinese and English, let one language own the title and body, and set the other as a support line at 0.75–0.9× that size — subtitle, label, or footnote — never a second equal column. Keep Chinese and English on separate lines or separate objects. Avoid topic-only titles such as "Update" or "背景".
- **Claim Discipline**: Keep facts, assumptions, implications, and requests semantically distinct. Cite the basis, period, and currency of every figure. Pair every recommendation with the action, owner, and date. Never promote an unsupported claim to a conclusion, and never bury the ask on a thank-you page.

## III. Page Role Vocabulary

| Role | Communication Job | Evidence Obligation | Composition Tendency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time-box agenda | Tell the room what will be covered in the allotted minutes | Name the decision or result, the minutes, and the two or three points the time will actually buy | Lead with the time box and the ask; treat the list as a contract, not a table of contents |
| Executive synthesis | State the governing decision and overall answer | Show the few supports that make the answer credible and identify any material gap | Lead with the answer, then group proof by supporting argument |
| Period result | Report the period against plan without rediscovering the business | Give actual, plan, and variance with units, period, and currency | Make variance, not raw value, the dominant fact |
| Recommendation / decision ask | Specify the action and what is needed from the room | Connect the action to diagnosis, effect, owner, date, and the cost of not deciding | Keep the ask dominant and its rationale visibly adjacent |
| Situation / complication | Establish the condition that makes the decision necessary | Distinguish observed conditions from the interpretation that creates the tension | Let the contrast between current reality and the answer carry the page |
| Options comparison | Clarify the decision through real alternatives | State basis, units, period, and comparability; never invent a benchmark | Align comparable evidence; emphasize only decision-relevant differences |
| IR / financial exhibit | Put the number that changes the decision in view | Retain definition, period, currency (`HK$`), source, and whether the figure is audited, management, or pro forma | Let the exhibit and its unit line dominate; keep commentary beside the number |
| Risk / mitigation | Expose uncertainty and the response it requires | Pair each risk with trigger, impact, mitigation, and owner when known | Keep risk and response in direct visual correspondence |
| Next steps / Q&A | Convert the meeting into owned action and an explicit question window | Give who / what / when for every item; leave a real Q&A region rather than a decorative close | One scannable action list; never end on thanks alone |
| Appendix / evidence | Preserve detail needed to audit or deepen the argument | Retain source, period, method, definitions, and limitations | Allow higher density while keeping the claim-to-source path legible |

## IV. Evidence & Data Expression

- **Argument Trace**: Every page message must trace back to one key support for the overall answer and forward to visible proof. Keep missing evidence or unresolved assumptions explicit instead of concealing the gap with confident wording.
- **Charts**: Choose the chart from the decision question and comparison logic. Use direct labels where practical, annotate the decision-relevant change, retain units and `HK$` or other declared currency, and remove legends, gridlines, or furniture that do not aid interpretation. Separate observation from interpretation and never invent a baseline, peer, target, or trend.
- **Tables**: Derive columns and row groups from the comparison or decision logic. Align units and periods, use Western thousand separators for HKD, preserve hierarchy, distinguish facts from assumptions, and emphasize only the differences that affect the answer. Use color only when it carries a declared meaning.
- **Sources**: Place citations close to the claim they support and retain source, period, scope, and measurement basis. Label estimates, proxies, and unaudited figures. Do not present unattributed statements as facts.
- **Native Editability**: Prefer editable native charts, tables, and business shapes when the supported interface fits the intended object and editability is useful. Otherwise retain a legible editable shape-based representation rather than sacrificing fidelity or meaning.

## V. Visual System Defaults

- **Preferred Visual Style**: swiss-minimal
- **Composition**: Build the page around the message-to-proof relationship. Favor a few primary content regions—normally one to three—while allowing more when the argument genuinely needs them. Maintain one clear scan path. Never split the page into equal Traditional Chinese and English columns; hierarchy, not symmetry, is the bilingual move.
- **Density**: Support dense business material through concise assertions, disciplined alignment, compact but legible labels, and whitespace that separates reasoning layers. Let synthesis, ask, and time-box pages breathe; let evidence and appendix pages tighten under a consistent grid without compressing below readable scale. A quarterly readout that cannot be spoken inside its time box is too long, not too important.
- **Decoration**: Use flat planes, hairline rules, restrained emphasis, and minimal elevation. Avoid ornamental cards, heavy gradients, floral or seal devices, decorative skyline silhouettes, and Mainland SOE red-gold chrome.
- **Color Behavior**: Start from a light paper field and reserve limited accents for semantic focus, change, risk, or the ask. Use coherent tints for related series instead of rainbow coding. Any confirmed Brand or Deck identity replaces these tendencies; never imply Brand Hong Kong, a listed-company house style, or a consultancy-specific palette.
- **Typography Character**: Use a compact professional sans-serif hierarchy suited to Traditional Chinese with a Latin companion. Assertion titles are decisive; support-language lines stay quieter and smaller. Do not apply English tracking, all-caps, or Latin display faces to Chinese. Increase Chinese body line-height relative to Latin. Exact families and locale coverage remain current-project or resolved identity decisions.

## VI. Image & Icon Direction

- **Preferred Image Rendering**: minimalist-swiss
- **Image Usage**: Use images only when they provide evidence, necessary context, or a causal explanation that shapes the decision. Default to sparse imagery; keep data and business structures editable instead of replacing them with decorative illustrations or stock Victoria Harbour photography.
- **Image Treatment**: Crop for the evidentiary subject, use restrained framing or a functional scrim, and retain a nearby caption or source when the image supports a claim. Avoid gratuitous full bleed, synthetic text inside images, and atmospheric skyline imagery that weakens the argument.
- **Icon Treatment**: Use one coherent stroke family with consistent weight, only when an icon clarifies a role, state, or relationship. Avoid logos, civic emblems, decorative icon grids, and mixed visual languages; actual icon selection remains a project decision.

## VII. Review Focus

> Apply this section only after the user explicitly activates visual review. It never triggers that stage.

- The intended answer is identifiable quickly at the rendered slide size.
- One language clearly dominates; the second language is support, not a 50/50 translation column.
- Traditional Chinese glyphs are used wherever Chinese is present; Simplified forms are absent unless the audience is explicitly Mainland.
- Visible evidence supports the message and is spatially connected to it.
- Facts, assumptions, implications, and requests remain distinguishable.
- Currency, period, and source remain legible on every exhibit.
- The time box or the ask is findable without hunting.
- Dense pages retain one clear scan path and do not hide overflow or structural ambiguity.
