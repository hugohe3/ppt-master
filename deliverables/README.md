# Pause Point — cinematic DMart pilot pitch

## Files

- **DMart_Pause_Point_Pilot_Pitch.pptx** — editable, animated 10-slide presentation with speaker notes on every slide.
- **DMart_Pause_Point_Pilot_Pitch.pdf** — static 10-page companion for reading and sharing. It does not contain transitions or builds.

## How to present

Download the PowerPoint and open it in a current desktop version of **Microsoft PowerPoint**. Start **Slide Show → From Beginning** (F5 on Windows). Click or press the right-arrow key to reveal the next controlled build or advance the slide. A few opening sequences play automatically within their slide; slides do not auto-advance. No sound effects are included.

GitHub previews, PDFs and other presentation applications may not reproduce the native PowerPoint motion. Older PowerPoint versions may substitute Fade for newer transitions.

## Design and motion

The cinematic redesign combines oversized editorial typography, a conceptual trolley schematic, forest/ivory act changes, source prototype evidence and a recurring pause mark. It retains the supplied palette: `#F2EFE9`, `#3A4C40`, `#8BA892`, `#C4694A`.

The PowerPoint contains **36 native object-animation rows**, with ordered reveals, Fly, Wipe, Fade and Faded Zoom builds. Three explicit Morph links preserve the pause mark across slides 1–3 and the source product view across slides 5–6. Fade, Push and Reveal transitions support the act changes. Text and schematic components remain editable.

The PDF embeds portable sans-serif text and uses a flat terracotta fill for the schematic seat in place of the PowerPoint's gradient.

## Evidence boundary

The attached Group 7 HCD presentation is the factual source; the palette PDF supplies visual direction only. Proposed commercial benefits and measurement plans are labeled as proposals, not results. The trolley illustration is a concept schematic, not an engineering drawing.

Outstanding `[NEEDED]` items include DMart-specific evidence, final mechanism-selection rationale, user and safety testing, KPI baselines and targets, measurement ownership and dates, pilot scope, investment and go/no-go criteria.
